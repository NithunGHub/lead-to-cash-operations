# 02 - Lead to Quote

The journey from a qualified opportunity to a quote the customer can sign. This is where pipeline discipline either pays off or gets exposed.

## Opportunity stages with exit criteria

Stages without exit criteria are labels. Each stage must answer: what must be true to move forward?

| Stage | Exit criteria |
|-------|---------------|
| Discovery | Pain confirmed, stakeholders identified, next step with a date |
| Qualification | Budget confirmed or disqualified, authority mapped, timeline real, fit validated |
| Solution / Demo | Technical validation complete, success criteria agreed |
| Proposal | Quote built from catalog pricing, economic buyer engaged |
| Negotiation | Contract in legal, commercial terms agreed in principle |
| Closed Won / Lost | All required fields complete; lost has a reason code |

Enforce with validation rules, not training decks. If a rep can move to Proposal without a quote attached, they will.

## Qualification rigor

Pick a framework and use it everywhere: MEDDPICC for enterprise complexity, a lighter variant for velocity.

- **Metrics:** Quantified pain and expected outcome.
- **Economic buyer:** Named, engaged before Proposal.
- **Decision process and criteria:** How they will decide, and what winning looks like.
- **Paper process:** Legal, security review, procurement mapped early, not discovered in Negotiation.
- **Champion:** Someone inside selling for you when you are not in the room.

Document these on the opportunity. A forecast built on opportunities with empty qualification fields is fiction.

## Multi-threading

Single-threaded deals die when the champion leaves or goes quiet. Require:

- Two or more engaged contacts before Proposal stage.
- Economic buyer identified (not necessarily engaged) before Negotiation.
- Contact roles assigned: champion, economic buyer, technical evaluator, end user.

## Forecasting from this pipeline

Forecast categories should derive from stage + qualification + close date realism:

- **Commit:** Paper process started, economic buyer confirmed, close date this period.
- **Best case:** Qualification complete, active evaluation, close date plausible.
- **Pipeline:** Everything else with a future close date.

Inspect weekly: stage age, close-date push history, and deals without next steps. A deal that has not moved in 30 days is not pipeline; it is hope.

## Common failure modes

- **Happy ears forecasting.** Reps forecast what they wish were true. Counter with exit criteria and inspection.
- **Sandbagging.** Opposite problem; counter with activity and coverage metrics, not accusations.
- **Stage stuffing.** Everything sits in Proposal. Counter with entry criteria, not just exit criteria.
- **Lost reasons unused.** If 80% of losses are "no decision," your qualification is broken upstream.
